## Overview

Phandalin is a community of farmers and prospectors rebuilt among the remains
of an older settlement. Mining in the nearby Sword Mountains is central to its
economy, alongside agriculture and woodland trades. The modern settlement began
to recover around 1486 DR and was established by 1491 DR. [Source: wiki history][history]

![Phandalin](Phandalin_ A Frontier Village Map.jpg)

## Geography and Layout

- Located in the Lost Hills of the Sword Mountains, south of Neverwinter Wood
  and northeast of Leilon.
- Associated with the route from the High Road toward Triboar, where the road
  becomes a trail. This description does not establish measured route lengths.
- Temperate climate; the article reports average annual rainfall of about
  18 inches / 46 centimeters, without establishing a project weather series.
- Icespire Peak is visible on clear days.
- The rebuilt village has roughly 50 log-and-stone structures. This is an
  approximate building count, not a count of houses or residents.
- An apple orchard lies to the west, with Tresendar Manor on a hill to the east.
  A town square and adjacent green form communal gathering spaces.

Source: [wiki description and geography][frwiki].

## Population and Society

The article describes a mainly human community with dwarves, elves, gnomes,
half-elves, and halflings. Farmers, woodcutters, trappers, and miners are among
the residents. It names Tymora and Waukeen in its religious overview, but their
specific shrines and temples belong to different periods.

**No numerical resident population was found in the consulted article.** The
engine's 500 residents are a modeling input, not a verified census. The roughly
50 structures cannot be converted into residents without knowing their uses,
occupancy, households, and whether outlying farms are included.

## Economy

The wiki describes suitable farming and ranching land and access to Neverwinter
Wood for logging. Gold and platinum mining are particularly important in its
late-15th-century account. Its combined trade summary also lists ore, timber,
skins, and cold iron; the cold-iron narrative includes the earlier resettlement
around 1374 DR and is not proof of an operating 1492 mine.

Edermath Orchard produces apples and cider. The Miner's Exchange weighs and
purchases prospectors' finds and keeps mining-claim records. Provisioning,
lodging, equipment sales, and local crafts support residents and travelers.

The later history describes increased tourism after Volo's visit, but also
disappointing mining income in subsequent years. These are dated developments,
not evidence for fixed annual visitor numbers or guaranteed mineral output.

**Engine comparison:** iron mining, silver mining, farming, and crafts are
configured locally. Silver ingots are the configured specialty. The consulted
article does not substantiate that silver specialization; it also does not
establish its absence. Gold, platinum, ranching, logging, and trapping warrant
review before any production changes. No output tonnages are supplied by the source.

Source: [wiki trade][trade] and [history][history].

## Government and Organizations

- The early rebuilt settlement had limited formal administration. The Miner's
  Exchange maintained mining claims and records.
- An annually elected townmaster acted as mediator, judge, and record keeper.
  The wiki identifies Harbin Wester as townmaster in 1491 DR.
- In the early 1490s, government changed to a three-member council: initially
  Harbin Wester, Sildar Hallwinter, and Trilena Stonehill. The article does not
  pin the transition to a precise date suitable for automatic 1492 selection.
- Halia Thornton later replaced Trilena; subsequent membership changes should
  remain historical annotations until their dates are established.
- In 1496 DR, a mayoral election and expansion toward five council members
  belong to the later setting, not the current planning baseline.
- Organizations connected with named residents and places include the Harpers,
  Zhentarim, Lionshield Coster, and, later, Acquisitions Incorporated.

The engine's ruler field is empty. Filling it requires a dated choice, not
copying the article's multi-period infobox wholesale. Source: [wiki government][government].

## Water and Infrastructure

The article describes **three deep wells**. This provides source support for
a water-source type and count, but not measured yield, potability in 1492,
ownership, delivery infrastructure, or daily collection capacity. The historical
ruins account includes a water-quality warning about one well; that earlier
condition must not be assumed to persist into the rebuilt settlement.

The location dashboard observed on 2026-10-01 labeled its water assessment
"inferred; hydrology unverified." It reported essential needs covered but a
routine-use gap. That is a simulation assessment, not a documented shortage.
The three-well evidence has not been connected to the runtime water model.

Townmaster's Hall provides civic space. The town square, green, orchard,
outlying farms, and manor are useful layout anchors, not a surveyed street plan.
No verified well yields, sewer network, road widths, or building footprints
have been established in this cross-reference. Source: [wiki][frwiki].

The wiki notes conflicting adventure chronology: a Mount Hotenow reference
suggests 1481, whereas its editorial convention dates *Lost Mine of Phandelver*
and its remake to 1491. It uses 1496 for *Acquisitions Incorporated*, explaining
that this year comes from a creator statement rather than an explicit date in
the sourcebook. Preserve these qualifications. Source: [wiki history and notes][history].

#### Refreshing the Snapshot

Run from the project root using the project Python environment:

```python
from pprint import pprint
from faerun.data_store import load_settlements
from faerun.requirements import settlement_profile

settlement = next(
  item for item in load_settlements() if item.id == "phandalin"
)
pprint(settlement_profile(settlement, include_services=True))
```

This reads stored inputs and computes the profile without changing the catalogue
or fetching wiki content. The tables are a dated snapshot, not automatically
synchronized documentation. Current location-detail responses already include
the engine profile under `requirements.profile`, separately from `lore`.
