## Welcome

Daggerford is a small walled town and duchy with ambitions out of proportion
to its size. It is the largest settlement between Waterdeep and Baldur's Gate,
and it has long tried to present itself as an alternative to Waterdeep: the
town recruits craftsmen and artisans to rival the City of Splendors and prides
itself on its guilds. Some travellers treat it as a mere stopover on the Trade
Way. Those who stay find a prosperous farming community, a busy river quay and
a castle older than the town itself.

Most of the duchy's people live on outlying hamlets, farms and estates rather
than inside the walls. The town is the market, the guildhall, the shipyard and
the seat of the dukes. It is mainly agricultural and largely self-sufficient,
though trade services are limited and prices for imported goods are often
marked up.

The name comes from the boy Tyndal, who in 931 DR is said to have killed six
lizardfolk raiders with a dagger while defending his family's trade wagon at the
river crossing. Every later duke has claimed descent from him. The town's arms,
a bloodied silver dagger on dark blue, remember the story.

- **By river:** Daggerford is a transfer point. The Delimbiyr is too shallow
  for ships to go further upstream, so cargo moves here between river boats and
  Trade Way caravans. Skippers and rivermen carry passengers downriver to
  Secomber and nearby settlements.
- **By caravan:** the Waterdeep-Daggerford Caravan Company runs scheduled
  wagons along the Trade Way (capacity 96,000 lb, 32 staff), and the Sword Coast
  Overland Exchange takes through consignments (capacity 144,000 lb, 44 staff).
  Both are Waterdeep houses that call here.
- **Within a day's travel:** Gillian's Hill, Liam's Hold and Black Helm Tower.
  The Floshin family estate is about two days away.
- **Market days:** an open market is held twice a tenday for grocers, farmers
  and other merchants.


## The land and the duchy

Daggerford stands on the Trade Way on the north bank of the Delimbiyr. The
Forgotten Realms Wiki notes that second- and third-edition sources put the town
south of the river, while fifth-edition maps place it on the north bank; this
guide follows the fifth-edition placement. The dukes claim land from the
Floshin Estates in the north to Dragonspear Castle in the south, but in
practice the duchy is the town and about a score of farmsteads.

The town is walled and has a moat, crossed at three gates: Farmers' Gate
(north), Caravan Gate (west) and River Gate (south). The moat was a refuse dump
until the late 15th century DR, and the streets are unpaved. The Ducal Castle
stands on a hill and is older than the town.

## History

- **133 DR:** Morlin Castle is built on the site and later abandoned. In 523 DR
  the area is part of Phalorm.
- **931 DR:** Tyndal's fight with the lizardfolk. He later marries into the ducal
  house of Calandor and, after a dragon destroys Delimbiyran around 947 DR,
  builds the Ducal Castle over the ruins of Morlin Castle.
- **Circa 1270 DR:** Duke Edwin dies defeating the devil Gorgomorz; his son
  Conan grants the town its charter.
- **Mid 13th century DR:** Clan Ironeater dwarves rebuild many of the town's 40-odd
  wooden buildings in stone.
- **Early 14th century DR:** the Council of Guilds is chartered (sources differ
  between 1280 and 1320 DR).
- **1363 DR:** the Battle of Daggerford against fiends from Dragonspear Castle.
- **1368 DR:** fifty militia march with the Coalition army in the Second
  Dragonspear War.
- **1372 DR:** Duke Pwyll's apparent death and a Zhentarim plot with the devil
  Gorgomorz; the merchant Matagar Bugo briefly rules, and Pwyll is restored (a
  video-game source).
- **Mid 15th century DR:** a sickness is blamed on the tanners, and the tannery
  is moved outside the walls, across the Trade Way. A silver discovery in the
  nearby Sword Hills swells the town and gives the Silver Flood Inn its name.
- **Circa 1485 to 1486 DR:** Duke Maldwyn is targeted by the succubus Pencheska
  and the spirit of the pit fiend Baazka. Primogeniture is suspended and Lady
  Morwen becomes duchess.
- **Circa 1489 DR:** a vampire attack, a rakshasa's portal and the release of the
  red dragon Karrnsyrrl damage the town.

*The wiki treats the state of the town in 1492 DR, after those events, as
unverified.*


## Government and defence

Daggerford is a duchy ruled by a duke or duchess of Tyndal's line. A **Council
of Guilds** of the town's guildmasters handles daily affairs. It was meant to be
anonymous, like Waterdeep's Lords, but everyone knows who sits on it. Each trade
has a guildmaster, even if the trade has only a single town representative, and
many guilds merged into fewer, larger ones by the 15th century DR. Daggerford
joined the Lords' Alliance before the Second Sundering.

**Rulers named by the wiki:** Conan (1320 DR), Pryden (1356 DR, killed in the First
Dragonspear War), Pwyll "Greatshout" (1370 DR), Maldwyn (1485 DR) and Morwen
(1489 DR). Around 1485 DR Duke Maldwyn was targeted by the succubus Pencheska,
and Lady Morwen may have been imprisoned in Cromm's Hold while Pencheska ruled
in her likeness. A "Lady Morwen" still ruled in 1489 DR; whether she was the
real duchess is unknown. The engine's ruler field is blank for the 1492 DR
baseline.

**Defence:** the duke's guard and a standing citizen militia (fifty strong in
1368 DR), serving three days a month with basic arms. The engine models
**8 standing soldiers** (one garrison company) and **63 militia**, which is
about 0.9% of the population under arms in the standing force.

**The Farmers' and Millers' Guild** is the engine's one active guild chapter
here. Its domains are farming, orchards, cattle, herding, brewing, vintning and
distilling, in the food trades. Chapter size 4, power
0.51, guild tax 0.47% and
enforcement 0.041.
The wiki also records guildhalls for the leatherworkers, tanners, carpenters,
animal handlers, farmers (over 200 members in and around the town), mariners,
watermen, jewelers, tailors, taverners, scribes, physicians and moneylenders.

## Economic profile

Daggerford is a farm town first. Its fields, orchards and herds feed the
duchy, and the surplus goes up and down the Trade Way. It also builds river
boats, forges tools and militia metalwork, tans hides, brews ale and makes furs
and garments. The lore names furs and garments as its exports and lumber, from
Andalor's Mill in Bowshot, as its import.

## Know before you come

- **Figures are a snapshot.** Prices and output are read from the live economy
  engine on 6 Marpenoth 1492 DR (autumn) and will drift with the seasons and with
  events. No events are active here today. The engine's seven-year price
  history for the town is not reproduced here.
- **Population is an estimate.** The gazetteer figure of 900 has no
  reference year; the wiki also gives 891 (1372 DR) and 1,200 (1485 DR).
- **Models, not surveys.** Workforce, hinterland, water, services and facility
  figures are estimated scenarios, not canon or a census.
- **Imports are not a true bill.** See the note under the economy table.
- **Dates differ by source.** Layout and business names mix the 1996 plan and
  later books. The wiki treats 1492 DR itself as unverified, including the state
  of the town after the events of about 1489 DR.
